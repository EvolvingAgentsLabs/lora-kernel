# Report: the original plan against what happened, and the pattern in the failures

**2026-09-16.** Written to be argued with. Every number is **[ran]** with its run
directory; claims about anything outside this repository are **[read]** and cited.

---

## 1. The plan, as written on 2026-09-07

> **Is acceptance against a frontier target a valid promotion criterion for a small
> expert — and how much verified quality is lost when the frontier is withdrawn?**

It rested on two claims, and the plan said only these two were worth money first:

1. **α means distillation.** A high acceptance rate against a frontier target
   identifies an expert that already produces what the frontier would, *here*.
2. **The frontier is removable.** Once α crosses a threshold in a region, the expert
   generates alone and the verified score barely moves. That difference — the
   **withdrawal gap** — is the product.

And it named its own falsification: *if α does not order candidates the way verified
quality does, claim 1 is false and the free router does not exist.*

## 2. What actually happened to each claim

| claim | verdict | where |
|---|---|---|
| **α means distillation** | **never priced.** Agreement picked the right expert on **9 of 10** decisive cases and recovered 0.725 of a 0.750 oracle — and **tied exactly** with a rule reading `clinic:` out of the prompt | S3 **[ran]** |
| **the frontier is removable** | **closed, but not by acceptance.** Adapter + calculator **40/40** = the teacher, withdrawal gap **0.000**. The adapter alone scores 4/40 and the base with the calculator 0/40 | P7 **[ran]** |
| **α as a metric at all** | **retired in characters.** Identical answers score 0.00 across formats; different answers 0.44 within one | C9 **[ran]** |
| **the frontier as scaffolding** | **inverted.** It is not withdrawn — it is the permanent fallback, worth **0.546 → 0.775** with 38% of cases leaving | P41 **[ran]** |
| **the frontier as speculative target** | **impossible, and always was.** No logprobs for a forced continuation, and a different tokenizer | C2, C3 · P48 **[ran]** |

**So the original question dissolved rather than being answered.** Not because α was
falsified — because the configuration it assumed (frontier as target, withdrawal as
the product) turned out not to be buildable, and the thing that *did* close the
withdrawal gap was twenty lines of arithmetic.

## 3. What was actually built, and it is not nothing

- **The substrate.** Two adapters resident on one base, selected by the `model` field
  of an HTTP request, measured **three times** including with a stranger's adapter
  beside ours **[ran]** P40/P41/P42.
- **One genuinely good expert.** `email-full`: **384/475 = 0.808**; on human messages
  **260/351 = 0.741** against a 0.655 bar, exact p **0.00036** **[ran]** P43.
- **Routing that pays, by region.** 0.546 → 0.775 **[ran]** P41.
- **The end-to-end inside a real agent**, with the inbox tools over MCP and zero
  requests leaving the machine for anything the pool serves **[ran]** P43.
- **A target chosen by hash rather than by argument** **[ran]** P48.

## 4. The pattern in the failures, which is the point of this report

Line up every negative result of the last ten days and the same thing is underneath
almost all of them.

| run | what we thought we were measuring | what we were actually measuring |
|---|---|---|
| S3 | whether agreement can route | whether the prompt already says the region — **it did** |
| P13 | whether a learned protocol beats code | whether copying one expression needs learning — **it does not** |
| P42 | whether a stranger's adapter helps | whether the base was already at the ceiling — **0.815 vs 0.825** |
| P45 | whether depth is what blocks the expert | whether the suite had any depth axis — **it had none; floor of six steps** |
| P46 | whether a typed head has room | the **information ceiling of the input** — 69-82% of the gap |
| P1 | whether a router is needed | whether twelve keywords do it — **1.000** |
| P49 | whether a bigger target is better | on one temática of three, **nothing** — both at the ceiling |

> **Almost every negative result in this project is the suite being wrong, not the
> architecture being wrong.**

And there is a reason it keeps happening, which is worth stating plainly:

> **Every suite here is generated by a function we wrote, and a generated suite
> cannot contain a difficulty we did not think of.**

We generate what we can verify mechanically. What can be verified mechanically tends
to be what a model already does — which is why the base keeps turning up at the
ceiling. Each time, the thing that would have made the experiment informative had
been designed out: the region was labelled, the difficulty was uniform, the decisive
fact was in the prompt, the temática was saturated.

**The repository has adapted by checking headroom first**, and that adaptation is
real — four arms have been cancelled before the GPU in two days, which is the system
working. But cancelling arms is not the same as producing results. **We are getting
very good at not wasting money and no better at finding a second useful expert.**

## 5. What is genuinely blocked, and it is one thing

**A second useful member of the pool.** Attacked from four directions and none held:

1. **Buy one.** The public Qwen2.5-3B ecosystem has personas without an oracle and
   MCQ adapters where the base is already at the ceiling **[ran]** P42.
2. **Narrow the failing expert.** No sub-region of it is competitive — every family
   dominated by the frontier **[ran]**.
3. **Fix its depth.** The floor is taught by the corpus, and below its band the
   expert over-solves on **18 of 18** **[ran]** P45.
4. **A typed head.** Cancelled by its own ceiling **[ran]** P46.

Everything downstream — the tournament, the router that picks a member, the verticals
— is waiting on this, and has been for nine days.

## 6. Speculative decoding: what NVIDIA's modules are, and what they are not

Read from [the collection][coll], [the blog][blog] and
[the Model-Optimizer README][mo] **[read]** 2026-09-16.

[coll]: https://huggingface.co/collections/nvidia/speculative-decoding-modules
[blog]: https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/an-introduction-to-speculative-decoding-for-reducing-latency-in-ai-inference/
[mo]: https://github.com/NVIDIA/Model-Optimizer/blob/main/examples/speculative_decoding/README.md

**What they are.** 18 modules, and the naming tells the story:
`Qwen3-235B-A22B-Eagle3`, `Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct-Eagle3`, `gpt-oss-120b-Eagle3-*`,
`Llama-3.1-8B-Medusa-FP8`, plus newer `DFlash`/`DSpark` heads. **Each one is a
drafting head bound to one specific large target.** Model-Optimizer trains them from
**the target's own hidden states**, and says nothing about reuse across targets —
which the input makes structurally unlikely **[read]**.

**The three variants.** Draft-target (a separate small model), **EAGLE-3** (a
lightweight head attached to the target's internal layers), and multi-token
prediction heads. The blog's only concrete figure is illustrative — three tokens in
250 ms against 600 ms — and it quotes **no acceptance rates and no speedups** for any
named model, which is worth noticing in a vendor introduction.

### The thing this settles for us, and it is a correction

**Speculative decoding has two possible purposes and we have been conflating them.**

| purpose | what wins | can our architecture claim it? |
|---|---|---|
| **latency** — make the big model fast | a head **trained on that target's hidden states** | **no** |
| **ranking** — which expert already produces what the big model would | **k domain experts drafting against one target** | **yes, and only this** |

An EAGLE-3 head exists for exactly our target — community builds of
`Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct_EAGLE3` are on the Hub **[read]** — and it will draft better
for that target than any of our domain experts ever will, because that is what it was
trained to do and it has no other job.

> **So our speculative decoding is not a latency play, and the documents should stop
> implying it is.** A 5 MB purpose-built head beats us at that. What it cannot do is
> **rank** — there is one head per target, so there is nothing to choose between.
> Acceptance as a **judge-free tournament metric over k experts** is the claim that
> survives, and it is the one thing this architecture has that EAGLE does not.

### And one thing worth taking

The two compose. **A 32B with an EAGLE head is a faster target**, and a faster target
makes the acceptance measurement cheaper — P49 spent most of an A100 hour waiting on
a 19.3 GB model. That is an engineering purchase, orthogonal to the thesis, and
available off the shelf.

~~**What we should not do** is train an EAGLE head ourselves. Model-Optimizer's
offline path wants *"several to tens of terabytes"* of hidden states; the online path
collocates draft and target. Neither is a workspace that rents an A100 by the hour.~~

**Corrected 2026-09-16 on the full README rather than a summary of it.** Two of those
sentences were too quick.

- **Qwen 2.5 is in the support matrix** for Medusa, EAGLE1/2 and EAGLE3 **[read]**.
  Not a community curiosity — a supported family.
- **The online path is for base models that fit in GPU memory**, and its worked
  example is Llama-3.2-1B. Ours is a **3B**. On the A100 this project already rents,
  that is feasible. The terabytes belong to the *offline* path, which is for models
  too large to collocate — and I generalised one path's cost to both.

What has not changed is that **an EAGLE head cannot rank experts**: there is one head
per target and nothing to choose between. It remains a latency purchase.

### The one thing in there that changes what we build

> *"To achieve higher acceptance rates during speculative decoding, it is beneficial
> to use conversations generated by the base model as training data. This ensures that
> the draft model's output distribution closely aligns with that of the base model."*
> **[read]**

That is about EAGLE drafts, and it **applies directly to our LoRA experts**. Every
corpus in this repository is generated from an **oracle** — the chain a correct solver
would write. If acceptance against a target is going to be the promotion criterion,
the corpus that produces the drafter should be generated **by the target**, not by an
oracle. Nobody here had connected those.

**And the subtlety is what makes it a ranking signal rather than a uniform one.** Each
expert trains on target-generated conversations **for its own region only**. Then
expert A agrees with the target on A, expert B on B, and acceptance varies by region —
which is exactly the signal the experiment needs. **If every expert trained on every
region's target output, acceptance would be uniform and the ranking would collapse.**
That is the failure mode to pre-register against in session 3.

### Two things worth not mistaking

- **Draft vocabulary compression (`d2t.pt`) does not bridge two families.** It maps a
  *smaller* draft vocab back to the target's own ids — it compresses one vocabulary,
  it does not reconcile two. `Qwen3.6-27B`'s 248,044 entries stay unusable as a target
  for our drafters.
- **`examples/specdec_bench` already validates acceptance** across vLLM, SGLang and
  TRT-LLM on a set of datasets **[read]**. This workspace's rule is to reuse an
  instrument rather than build one; if we ever measure acceptance as a speed claim,
  that is where it should be measured rather than in something written here.

## 7. What to change, stated as options rather than a decision

1. **Stop generating the suite where the difficulty matters.** Every failure in §4 is
   a suite we wrote. The one place with difficulty we did not design is the
   **OpenClaw end-to-end** — a real agent, a real tool surface of 54, a real failure
   nobody planned. The 3-tools-versus-54 blocker is the first honest difficulty this
   project has met, and it arrived by accident.
2. **Make the headroom check a gate in the runner, not a habit.** It has been run
   four times by hand in two days. A step that cannot state its ceiling should not be
   launchable.
3. **Accept that the second expert may have to be bought, not found.** Three of the
   four attempts in §5 were searches. The fourth — building a corpus that teaches a
   policy the base demonstrably lacks — is the only one that has ever worked, and
   P49 just measured exactly such a gap: the base omits the reference **28 times of
   30** and the amount 25.
4. **Rank the claims by what would survive publication.** The substrate, the one good
   expert and region routing are real. The tournament, the layers and the three tiers
   are analysis. The gap between those two lists is the honest state of the project.
